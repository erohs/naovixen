## The problem

People upload a lot of audio and video into their portfolios, but none of it could carry captions or transcripts. That was a WCAG 2.2 conformance gap, and it came up again and again in customer accessibility audits and tender questionnaires.

## My approach

I built a streaming WebVTT parser in C#/.NET so large caption files never sit in memory, then a set of reusable React and TypeScript components: a caption track for the player, a transcript that follows playback, and an upload flow for caption files.

## Challenges

Caption files in the wild are messy: missing headers, overlapping cues and odd encodings. Being strict broke real files, but being loose let bad data through to the player.

<Callout label="lesson learnt" title="Be forgiving on input, strict on output">
    The parser recovers from common mistakes and logs them, but everything it hands to the UI is validated and
    sanitised.
</Callout>

## Outcome

Shipped across three products. Captions and transcripts closed a recurring audit finding and gave the sales team a clear answer in tenders.

## What I'd do differently

Run sessions with deaf and hard-of-hearing users before designing the transcript panel, rather than validating it afterwards.
